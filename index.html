08 / INVESTMENT THINKING

Investor frameworks

Four distinct ways to pressure-test a company before trusting the numbers.

ANALYST CHECKLISTS
Use the frameworks as questions, not as borrowed conviction.

These are concise summaries of well-known investing disciplines. They are not quotes, endorsements, personal recommendations, or simulations of the investors. ModelForge's assistant remains a local, deterministic tool grounded in the active model.

VALUE · MARGIN OF SAFETY

Benjamin Graham

Start with what the business owns and earns, not the story. Estimate conservative value, test the downside, and demand a meaningful gap between value and price.

  • What is normalized earning power?
  • What does the balance sheet protect?
  • How much margin of safety remains?
QUALITY · CAPITAL ALLOCATION

Warren Buffett

Look for durable competitive advantages, strong economics, understandable businesses, and management that allocates capital rationally over time.

  • Are returns on capital high and durable?
  • Can the business reinvest at attractive rates?
  • Do reported earnings convert into cash?
INVERSION · AVOIDING ERROR

Charlie Munger

Invert the problem. Instead of asking only how the company wins, identify what could break the thesis, destroy capital, or make the forecast fragile.

  • What could permanently impair the business?
  • Which incentives or dependencies are dangerous?
  • What evidence would disprove the thesis?
BUSINESS UNDERSTANDING · GROWTH

Peter Lynch

Understand how the company makes money, identify the real growth driver, and test whether the growth and valuation assumptions are still reasonable.

  • Can the business model be explained simply?
  • What evidence supports the growth runway?
  • Does the valuation already price in success?
